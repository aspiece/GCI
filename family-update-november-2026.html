Snack donations support financial literacy

If your family is able, please consider donating individually packaged, shelf-stable snacks for our classroom store.

We use ClassBank, a digital classroom economy, as part of our financial literacy work. Students earn virtual classroom dollars through classroom jobs and positive contributions, then practice deciding how much to save or spend. Snacks are one of the incentives students can purchase with their classroom dollars. This connects budgeting, saving, and spending decisions with their effort and responsibility in class.

Students can bring donations to class. Contributions are optional, and any amount is appreciated. Thank you for helping make these lessons meaningful!

Software Engineering team project and competition

Students in our Software Engineering program are working in teams on a classroom project that culminates in the Aerial Drone Competition. Each student is expected to fulfill their team responsibilities, complete their share of the work, and communicate with teammates as they prepare, program, test, and document their drone. The competition gives students an opportunity to apply that work together and is an essential part of our Career Technical Education program.

  • Tentative date: Saturday, February 13, 2027, at Kettering University. This date remains tentative until our registration is confirmed.
  • Meeting place: Everyone will meet at Kettering’s Connie and Jim John Recreation Center, 1700 University Avenue, Flint, MI 48504. We’ll share our exact meeting spot and arrival time once confirmed.

Please review your family’s February schedule with your student over Thanksgiving break. Have your student speak with me privately about any conflicts as soon as we return. Knowing early is crucial for planning: teammates are counting on each student to do their part.

Student Helpdesk and computer donations

Our Student Helpdesk gives students hands-on experience troubleshooting computer problems and making repairs. If you have an out-of-warranty computer or device that needs attention, we’re happy to take a look!

We also accept donations of old computers for students to troubleshoot, repair, and learn from. These projects help students build practical IT support skills while helping our community.

Please email Dominic Cascarelli or Andrew Spiece to discuss a device or arrange a donation.

Our student-run 3D print farm

Our student-run print farm now has 12 printers and can handle larger orders as well as individual items. Students gain hands-on experience managing orders, operating printers, and producing finished prints.

Browse MakerWorld for 3D models to find something you’d like printed. We currently accept ready-to-print files only; custom design orders are not available.

Current pricing:

  • Printer time: $1.50 per hour.
  • Filament: $20 per kilogram of material used, in addition to printer time.
  • Larger orders: Discounts are available.

Our quote system is not yet up and running. Please encourage your student to submit an order through the Print Lab submission form.

Open the Print Lab Submission Form

Every order supports our students. All funds collected help cover competition travel, apparel, and gear for competitions and Work Based Learning. Our goal is for students to participate in the program without having to pay out of pocket.

Activate your GCI ParentVUE account

ParentVUE lets you check GCI attendance, progress reports, and report cards. GCI uses a separate ParentVUE login from your child’s home school district.

  1. Request an activation key from GCI@geneseeisd.org or the GCI main office at 810-591-4462.
  2. Open GCI ParentVUE and choose More Options → Activate Account.
  3. Enter the name and activation key provided by the school, then create your username and password.

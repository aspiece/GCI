How infrastructure changes the picture

Your choices affect how often AI is used, but the data center's design can also make a big difference. The same AI task can have very different environmental impacts depending on how the data center is cooled and powered.

Two types of water use Using less water at the data center does not always mean an AI system has zero water impact. Electricity generation can also require water.

Direct water is used at the data center itself — mainly for cooling servers.
Indirect water is used off-site to generate the electricity the data center needs (for example, to cool power-plant condensers or produce steam).

What makes the difference?

AI's environmental impact depends on more than how many prompts you send. These five factors shape the total footprint:

  • Number and type of AI requests — more complex tasks take more computing power.
  • Computing hardware and efficiency — newer chips often do more work per watt.
  • Cooling technology — closed-loop liquid cooling uses far less water than evaporative cooling towers.
  • Source of electricity — renewable energy produces less CO₂ and can require less water than fossil fuels.
  • Data center location and climate — cooler climates and regions with cleaner grids have lower footprints.
What you can do — and what companies can do Your choices can reduce unnecessary AI use, but infrastructure matters too. More efficient cooling and cleaner electricity can greatly reduce the impact of the same AI task. Using AI intentionally — and avoiding unnecessary retries — is still a good habit, but the biggest gains come from better data center design.
